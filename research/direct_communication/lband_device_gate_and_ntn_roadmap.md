## 0. Answer first: the device gate, stated plainly

**THE DEVICE-GATE VERDICT. Iridium's L-band (1616-1626.5 MHz) can be received today only by PURPOSE-BUILT hardware carrying a dedicated Iridium modem and antenna: sat phones, Certus terminals, SBD/IoT modules, push-to-talk radios, and consumer messengers (Garmin inReach, Zoleo, Bivy) that each embed an Iridium module. NONE is a standard smartphone. On the roadmap, Iridium's own direct-to-device path (Project Stardust / Iridium NTN Direct) is 3GPP NB-IoT NTN, a NARROWBAND messaging-and-IoT standard (kbps-class, ~1 to 2 kbps peak per the NTN NB-IoT envelope), demonstrated on a purpose-built Nordic nRF9151 IoT module, NOT the modem in a mainstream phone. The decisive structural fact: Iridium's 1616-1626.5 MHz TDD block is NOT a deployed 3GPP NTN band. The standardized NTN bands are n255 (L-band, 1626.5-1660.5 up / 1525-1559 down) and n256 (S-band), which are the Inmarsat/Ligado/Globalstar-style MSS blocks that every shipping NTN chipset targets; Iridium's block sits just BELOW n255 and, though it falls inside n254's uplink range, n254 is an FDD band that pairs that uplink with a Globalstar S-band downlink, whereas Iridium runs TDD in the single band both ways. Iridium's own standardization (a new NB-IoT NTN work item in 3GPP Release 19, frozen Q4 2025) still requires device makers to REDESIGN hardware to add the band, which Iridium itself acknowledges. The proof point already happened: the Qualcomm-Iridium Snapdragon Satellite silicon (2023) was built and demonstrated, then got ZERO phone adoption and was killed in November 2023, because OEMs chose the standards-based n255/n256 path. So the mainstream-phone door on Iridium's native L-band is closed today and stays closed on the roadmap regardless of how many satellites fly, until and unless a phone maker adds Iridium's specific band to new silicon, which no one has committed to. Iridium's addressable device population is the purpose-built-module base (sat terminals + NB-IoT-NTN IoT modules), not the billions of unmodified handsets.**

The five load-bearing pieces, each sourced below:

1. **The device landscape today (Section 1).** Every Iridium-L-band device is purpose-built: sat phones (Iridium 9555 ~$1,295, Extreme 9575 ~$1,349, voice at ~2.4 kbps), Certus terminals (9810 peak 704 kbps down / 352 kbps up; 9770 midband ~88/22 kbps; ~$2,000+ terminals), SBD/IoT modules (9602 ~$325, 9603 ~$140, 340-byte messages), PTT radios (Extreme PTT ~$1,799), and consumer messengers (inReach Mini 2 ~$400, Zoleo ~$200, Bivy Stick ~$300, each embedding an Iridium SBD modem). A bare smartphone can do none of it, because it has no L-band MSS radio. [FACT]

2. **Project Stardust, pinned (Section 2).** Iridium NTN Direct is 3GPP NB-IoT NTN (narrowband, NOT NR-NTN broadband); a software upgrade to the 66-satellite L-band fleet; services are messaging, SOS, and IoT (NO voice); the rate is kbps-class (~1 to 2 kbps peak, standard NTN NB-IoT envelope; Iridium publishes no specific figure); the lead chipset is Nordic Semiconductor's nRF9151 IoT module; on-air two-way messaging trials began January 2026 ("To Iridium and Beyond"); commercial service targeted 2026. It requires new/purpose-built NB-IoT hardware, not a software toggle on existing phones. [FACT]

3. **The 3GPP NTN band map (Section 3).** The deployed FR1 NTN bands are n255 (L) and n256 (S), Rel-17, FDD, targeted by every shipping NTN chipset (Qualcomm X80/X85/9205S/212S, MediaTek MT6825, Nordic nRF9151). Iridium's 1616-1626.5 MHz is OUTSIDE these: below n255, overlapping n254's uplink but not its FDD pairing, and TDD where the standard bands are FDD. No mainstream phone modem lists Iridium's band. [FACT]

4. **The structural ceiling (Section 4).** L-band MSS cannot carry decent-video rates (~2 to 3 Mbps per active user) to an unmodified phone, on three independent grounds: standardization (Iridium chose the NARROWBAND NB-IoT NTN track, not NR-NTN broadband, and its band is in neither's deployed band list), chipset adoption (no phone maker has added Iridium's band; the Qualcomm attempt failed), and link physics (10.5 MHz total, power-limited, and the chosen NB-IoT waveform is kbps-class by design). Of the three real device paths flying today, Iridium's L-band NTN most resembles Skylo/NB-IoT (purpose-built modules), NOT Starlink/T-Mobile (unmodified phones on borrowed cellular). [FACT/DERIVED]

5. **The service-tier ladder (Section 5).** The model input: per-user rates by tier, from SBD (bytes/message) and voice (~2.4 kbps) through NB-IoT NTN (~1 to 2 kbps peak, tens of kbps ideal), Certus mid (~88 kbps), Certus 700 (704 kbps), to hypothetical video-grade (~2 to 3 Mbps). Every tier below video is deliverable ONLY to a purpose-built Iridium device; the video tier is not deliverable on Iridium L-band at all, and none is deliverable to a standard unmodified phone. [DERIVED]

**Confidence: high on the device gate and the band-map spine** (the band edges are 3-source FACT, the TDD-vs-FDD distinction and the Qualcomm-Iridium failure are multi-source FACT, the device landscape is multi-source). **High on the Stardust characterization** (NB-IoT NTN, no voice, Nordic nRF9151, 2026 all multi-source; the specific kbps is the standard envelope, not an Iridium claim, and is flagged as such). **The structural-ceiling conclusion is a well-supported synthesis**, not a single sourced number. No verdict on the Rocket Lab business is rendered.

## 2. Project Stardust (Iridium NTN Direct), pinned precisely

Project Stardust (announced Jan 10, 2024; rebranded "Iridium NTN Direct" Sept 2024) is Iridium's direct-to-device path over its existing 66-satellite L-band fleet (COMM-614). Here it is pinned to the specifics the device question turns on.

### 2.1 The standard: NB-IoT NTN (narrowband), Release 19 for Iridium's work item

Iridium NTN Direct is **3GPP NB-IoT NTN (narrowband IoT, non-terrestrial network), NOT NR-NTN (5G broadband)**; every source is unanimous on "NB-IoT / narrowband" [FACT]. On the release number, two figures are both correct and mean different things:
- The NB-IoT NTN *feature* first appeared in **3GPP Release 17** (the first NTN release) [FACT].
- Iridium's *own specific inclusion* (its L-band NB-IoT NTN work item) was accepted into **Release 19**, frozen ~Q4 2025 [FACT].

So Rel-17 = the feature's origin; Rel-19 = Iridium's accepted work item. Sources: [Iridium 3GPP acceptance PR, Sept 25 2024](https://investor.iridium.com/2024-09-25-Iridium-Accelerates-Direct-to-Device-Service-with-Acceptance-into-3GPP-Standards-Announces-Iridium-NTN-Direct); [SpaceNews Iridium Rel-19 work item](https://spacenews.com/iridium-approved-to-work-on-leo-compatibility-for-upcoming-smartphones); [Via Satellite NTN Direct](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2024/09/25/iridium-plans-standards-based-service-ntn-direct/); [Developing Telecoms](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/satellite-communications-networks/16046-iridium-to-support-direct-3gpp-based-5g-iot-connections-by-2026.html). (This confirms and sharpens the corpus's "Release 19" in COMM-614.)

### 2.2 The data rate: kbps-class (Iridium publishes no specific figure)

Iridium has not stated a throughput number for NTN Direct, so no specific kbps should be attributed to Iridium [UNKNOWN, Iridium-specific]. At the STANDARD level, NB-IoT NTN over satellite is constrained to roughly **1 to 2 kbps peak** with a maximum transport block of ~256 bytes and latency in the tens of seconds, versus tens-to-hundreds of kbps for terrestrial NB-IoT ([Ground Control NTN NB-IoT Q&A](https://www.groundcontrol.com/blog/your-top-questions-about-ntn-nb-iot-answered/)) [FACT, standard-level]. Independent NTN performance analysis gives ~2.3 kbps per HARQ process (up to ~4.6 kbps with two) in LEO scenarios, and ~20 to 60 kbps downlink only in ideal conditions ([MDPI Sensors, NB-IoT uplink in LEO NTN](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/18/7097); search-corroborated NB-IoT NTN envelope) [FACT, standard-level]. The read for the model: **messaging and tiny-telemetry grade, kbps-class, small-packet**, not a broadband pipe. This is the Iridium NTN Direct tier's ceiling by the standard it chose.

### 2.3 The services: messaging, SOS, IoT (NO voice)

Iridium's own framing is "5G NTN messaging and SOS capabilities for smartphones, tablets, cars, and related consumer applications," with the Jan 2026 trial listing emergency/safety messaging, asset tracking, automotive/transportation monitoring, utilities, logistics, agriculture, and remote infrastructure [FACT]. **No voice** is in NTN Direct; the service is limited to low-data-rate applications (sensor data, text messages) [FACT]. (The Sept 2024 rebrand release's phrase "global voice and data" describes Iridium's OVERALL company network, its separate legacy L-band voice product, not NTN Direct; do not attribute voice to NTN Direct.) Iridium's existing network already handles ~1,300 SOS/911-equivalent incidents per year, the backbone it extends to NTN Direct devices [FACT]. Sources: [Iridium Stardust PR, Jan 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/iridium-unveils-project-stardust-302030534.html); [Verdict Project Stardust](https://www.verdict.co.uk); [StockTitan IRDM Jan 2026](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/IRDM).

### 2.4 The chipset and partners: Nordic nRF9151, standards-based (Qualcomm is out)

- **Lead chipset: Nordic Semiconductor nRF9151** (a System-in-Package: LTE-M / NB-IoT / NB-NTN / DECT NR+), a low-power IoT module for battery devices. The Jan 2026 on-air test's first mobile-originated messages used the nRF9151 [FACT]. NOT the nRF9161. Nordic shipped NTN modem firmware and an nRF9151 dev kit in Dec 2025 ([Nordic nRF9151](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/nRF9151); [StockTitan IRDM Jan 2026](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/IRDM)).
- **Qualcomm is OUT**: the prior proprietary Qualcomm Snapdragon Satellite deal (Jan 2023) was terminated Nov 2023 because "device makers indicated a preference for standards-based D2D solutions"; Stardust is the standards-based replacement [FACT] ([Verdict](https://www.verdict.co.uk); Section 3.5). No MediaTek or Sony/Altair partnership for Iridium is public [UNKNOWN].
- **Roaming / MNO partners**: Deutsche Telekom (Sept 2025), Vodafone IoT (Nov 2025), Karrier One [FACT] ([Iridium Vodafone, Nov 2025](https://investor.iridium.com); [ComSoc DT](https://techblog.comsoc.org)).
- **No specific launch device** (no named phone or car model) has been announced [UNKNOWN].

### 2.5 The device question: purpose-built modules now, future phones only with new silicon

**Iridium NTN Direct requires purpose-built NB-IoT hardware, not a software toggle on existing phones** [FACT]. The demonstrated device is a Nordic nRF9151 IoT module, not the modem in a mainstream smartphone. For phones specifically, SpaceNews is explicit that this is a FUTURE-phone capability: "Updated chipset standards due for release late next year could enable mass-market smartphones to connect with Iridium's satellites," via "industry-standard chips" manufacturers would integrate, i.e. upcoming devices with new silicon and RF front-ends, not today's installed base and not an over-the-air update [FACT] ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/iridium-approved-to-work-on-leo-compatibility-for-upcoming-smartphones)). No mainstream iPhone or Galaxy today includes a radio for Iridium's L-band block [FACT/UNKNOWN-absent] (Section 3).

**Status (2026):** on-air two-way messaging trials began Jan 21, 2026 (test message "To Iridium and Beyond") over the live LEO fleet using the nRF9151; next is beta, with commercial service targeted 2026 [FACT] ([Iridium investor, Jan 21 2026](https://investor.iridium.com/2026-01-21); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/IRDM)).

## 3. The 3GPP NTN band map, and where Iridium's band is not

This is the decisive layer. If Iridium's specific band is not in the standardized NTN band plan that chipsets implement, the phone door stays closed regardless of fleet size.

### 3.1 The deployed FR1 NTN bands (n255, n256) and the others

The FR1 (sub-7-GHz) NTN band definitions, confirmed identically across three independent references ([RF Wireless World NTN bands](https://www.rfwireless-world.com/terminology/ntn-frequency-bands); [5G-Tools NR-NTN bands](https://5g-tools.com/frequency-bands-for-5g-nr-ntn/); [Hubble NB-NTN bands n249/n255/n256](https://hubble.com/community/guides/how-3gpp-nb-ntn-frequency-bands-n249-n255-n256-work-for-satellite-iot/)) [FACT]:

| Band | Uplink (MHz) | Downlink (MHz) | Duplex | Rel | Spectrum holder / user |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **n255** | **1626.5-1660.5** | **1525-1559** | FDD | 17 | Inmarsat / Ligado L-band MSS |
| **n256** | **1980-2010** | **2170-2200** | FDD | 17 | 2 GHz MSS (Globalstar-adjacent / EchoStar) |
| n254 | 1610-1626.5 | 2483.5-2500 | FDD | 18 | UL overlaps Iridium; DL is Globalstar S-band (= n53) |
| n249 | 399.9-400.05 | 387.0-387.15 | FDD | (IoT NTN) | UHF satellite IoT (~150 kHz, one NB-IoT carrier) |
| n510/n511/n512 | 27.5-30 GHz | 17.7-20.2 GHz | FDD | 18 | Ka-band VSAT/GEO |

(Wikipedia's table also lists L/S-band variants n250-n253 as spec placeholders, single-source; Ku-band NTN is planned for Rel-19. These do not change the picture.) NB-IoT NTN and NR-NTN share these FR1 bands; the difference is the waveform (narrowband vs 5G NR), not the band. Both n255 and n256 are FDD (separate paired uplink/downlink frequencies), and the current maximum NTN FR1 channel bandwidth is 20 MHz ([Techplayon 3GPP satellite bands](https://www.techplayon.com/3gpp-frequency-bands-for-5g-satellite-communication/)) [FACT].

### 3.2 Iridium's 1616-1626.5 MHz is OUTSIDE the deployed NTN bands (the decisive point)

Iridium's L-band block is **1616-1626.5 MHz**, used TDD (both directions in the one block). Place it against the table:
- It sits **just BELOW n255**, whose uplink STARTS at 1626.5 MHz (Iridium's top edge). So Iridium is NOT in n255. [FACT, from the edges above]
- It falls INSIDE **n254's uplink** range (1610-1626.5 MHz), but this does NOT make Iridium "n254," because n254 is an **FDD** band that pairs that uplink with a SEPARATE downlink at 2483.5-2500 MHz, which is Globalstar's S-band (the same block as Apple's Band 53). Iridium instead runs **TDD** in 1616-1626.5 both ways. No standardized 3GPP band replicates Iridium's TDD-in-L-band arrangement. [FACT]
- Published research treats the two as NEIGHBORS/INTERFERERS, not the same band: a 2025 study analyzes co-channel interference from future n254 D2D NTN user terminals INTO Iridium's uplink ([ACM, "Analysis of Co-channel Interference Between Future D2D NTN Systems and Iridium System in L-Band," 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-95299-9_14)) [FACT-SS]. That is the literature confirming Iridium's band is a distinct arrangement adjacent to the standardized bands, not one of them.

So Iridium's spectrum is outside the DEPLOYED NTN bands, both by frequency (below n255) and by duplexing (TDD where the standard bands are FDD).

### 3.3 Which chipsets support which NTN bands (all target n255/n256, none Iridium)

Every shipping NTN chipset targets n255 (L) and/or n256 (S), the Inmarsat/Ligado/Globalstar-style MSS blocks, NOT Iridium's 1616-1626.5 [FACT]:
- **Qualcomm**: Snapdragon X80 (first 5G modem with integrated NB-NTN) and X85 do NB-NTN; the IoT modems 9205S / 212S explicitly list "R17 IoT NTN, n255 (L), n256 (S)" ([Qualcomm X80](https://www.qualcomm.com/modems/products/snapdragon-x80-5g-modem-rf-system); [Skylo certified devices](https://www.skylo.tech/certified-devices)).
- **MediaTek MT6825**: the first R17 5G NTN chip, "two-way satellite via L- and S-bands," in Bullitt / Motorola Defy Satellite Link, certified on Skylo ([MediaTek MT6825](https://www.mediatek.com/technology/5g/5g-satellite-ntn/mt6825)).
- **Nordic nRF9151**: R17 NB-NTN, supports "n255 (1.6 GHz L-band) and n256 (2 GHz S-band)," Skylo-certified Dec 2025 ([Nordic/Skylo cert](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Nordic-news/2025/12/Nordic-Semiconductor-secures-Skylo-certification-for-nRF9151-module-enabling-global-IoT-connectivity)).

The critical nuance for Iridium: the SAME nRF9151 module that Iridium uses for NTN Direct is a generic n255/n256 NB-NTN part; to reach Iridium's specific band it runs Iridium's Rel-19 NB-IoT NTN arrangement with Iridium's own RAN (Gatehouse) and network. The chipset's native band list is n255/n256, and Iridium's 1616-1626.5 is a distinct addition Iridium's program enables, not a band a stock phone-modem ships with.

### 3.4 The roadmap: Iridium's band is being standardized, but still needs new device hardware

Trade press is explicit: "manufacturers will still have to redesign 5G devices to use Iridium's L-band frequencies (1616 and 1626.5 MHz)" [FACT] ([Developing Telecoms](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-technology/satellite-communications-networks/16046-iridium-to-support-direct-3gpp-based-5g-iot-connections-by-2026.html)). I could not confirm a specific "nXXX" number assigned to Iridium's block [UNKNOWN, exact band number]. There is NO announced plan by Qualcomm, MediaTek, or Apple to add Iridium's 1616-1626.5 MHz to a mainstream smartphone modem [FACT/UNKNOWN-absent]. Iridium's device story rides dedicated IoT modules (nRF9151-class) plus its own transceivers, not flagship phone chipsets.

## 4. The structural ceiling: can L-band MSS ever carry decent-video rates to an unmodified phone?

The founder's maximum-outcome question, answered on the chain the corpus uses (standardization, chipset adoption, link physics). Decent video = ~2 to 3 Mbps per ACTIVE user.

### 4.1 Standardization: Iridium chose the narrowband track, and its band is in neither broadband band list

There are two NTN tracks: **NR-NTN** (5G New Radio, broadband D2D, higher rates, can target smartphones) and **NB-IoT NTN** (narrowband, kbps-class, low-power IoT) ([RF Wireless World NR vs IoT NTN](https://www.rfwireless-world.com/terminology/difference-between-5g-nr-and-iot-ntn); [Techplayon NB-IoT NTN vs 5G NR NTN](https://www.techplayon.com/nb-iot-ntn-vs-5g-nr-ntn/)) [FACT]. Iridium chose **NB-IoT NTN**, the narrowband one, by design a messaging/IoT standard, not a broadband one (Section 2.1). Even the broadband track (NR-NTN) is standardized on the n255/n256 (and Ka) bands, NOT on Iridium's 1616-1626.5 (Section 3.2). So there is no standardized broadband-NTN path over Iridium's specific band today: the broadband standard exists, but not on Iridium's frequency, and Iridium's own program is the narrowband standard. [DERIVED on cited facts]

### 4.2 Chipset adoption: no one has added Iridium's band, and the one attempt failed

A broadband-to-phone service would need phone modems that both (a) implement NR-NTN and (b) include an RF front-end for Iridium's 1616-1626.5 MHz. No shipping or announced phone modem does (b) (Section 3.3-3.4), and the only serious attempt to put Iridium's band into phone silicon (Qualcomm Snapdragon Satellite) was killed in 2023 for lack of OEM uptake (Section 3.5). Adding a new L-band front-end and antenna tuning to a mass-market phone is a hardware-cost and board-area decision phone makers make only for capabilities they believe will sell in volume; they made it for the standardized n255/n256 (Skylo ecosystem) and for Apple's captive Globalstar path, not for Iridium's band. [DERIVED]

### 4.3 Link physics: 10.5 MHz, power-limited, narrowband by construction

Iridium holds 10.5 MHz total at L-band (COMM-611), and the whole band runs the low-rate TDD waveform. Video-grade ~2 to 3 Mbps per active user is two to three orders of magnitude above the NB-IoT NTN envelope (~1 to 2 kbps peak, ~20 to 60 kbps ideal, Section 2.2). Even setting aside the standard, the bare-phone link is power-limited (the phone supplies no gain), which is exactly why the corpus's cellular product (lane 2) needs a large aperture PLUS ~20 to 40 MHz of owned CELLULAR spectrum to reach ~25 to 50 Mbps to one phone (COMM-347). Iridium's L-band gives neither a phone-tunable cellular band nor more than 10.5 MHz, and its chosen waveform is narrowband. So the link physics and the spectrum width both cap Iridium's L-band far below video-to-a-bare-phone. [DERIVED on COMM-611, COMM-347, COMM-665]

- **The concurrency dimension (how many at once).** The ladder's rates are per-user; the CONCURRENT user capacity is bounded separately. Voice: 2.4 kbps AMBE vocoder circuits at ~80 circuits per beam, ~1,100 concurrent calls per satellite, ~172,000 concurrent constellation-wide [FACT] ([Wikipedia: Iridium satellite constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iridium_satellite_constellation); [highspeedsat Iridium](https://www.highspeedsat.com/iridium-satellite.php)); this upgrades the corpus's single-source ~1,100-calls note (in COMM-610's context) to two-source. NB-IoT NTN: capacity is RANDOM-ACCESS-COLLISION limited (the contention/access channel saturates before the bandwidth does), so the NTN Direct tier's concurrent-user count is bounded by access attempts, not by held MHz [FACT-SS] ([arXiv 2406.14107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14107)). So the max-outcome chain is: device-gated first (who can receive the band at all), then concurrency-capped (how many at once per beam and per satellite). [FACT + DERIVED]

## 6. So what (for the Iridium max-outcome question)

1. **The device is the binding gate, and it is closed to unmodified phones on Iridium's native band.** Every Iridium-L-band device today is purpose-built; a standard phone has no radio for the band (COMM-661, COMM-482). The fleet size does not change this. [FACT]
2. **Iridium's own direct-to-device path is narrowband, to purpose-built modules.** Project Stardust / NTN Direct is NB-IoT NTN (messaging/SOS/IoT, kbps-class, no voice), demonstrated on a Nordic nRF9151 IoT module, service targeted 2026. Mainstream phones would need FUTURE new silicon that no one has committed to (COMM-664, COMM-666). [FACT]
3. **The decisive structural fact is the band map.** Iridium's 1616-1626.5 MHz TDD block is not a deployed 3GPP NTN band; the standard bands are n255/n256 (FDD, Inmarsat/Ligado/Globalstar-style), which every shipping chipset targets and Iridium's band is not among. The Qualcomm-Iridium failure (2023) is the live proof (COMM-668, COMM-669, COMM-670). [FACT]
4. **The video-to-a-bare-phone ceiling is hard on three grounds.** Standardization (narrowband track, band not in either track's list), chipset adoption (no one has added the band), and link physics (10.5 MHz, power-limited, narrowband). Iridium's path resembles Skylo/NB-IoT (purpose modules), not Starlink/T-Mobile (unmodified phones) (COMM-671, COMM-672, COMM-673). [DERIVED]
5. **The model input is the service-tier ladder and the device-bounded customer set.** Rates run bytes/message (SBD) to 704 kbps (Certus 700), with NTN Direct kbps-class; no video tier; every tier purpose-built; the addressable population is the sat-terminal + IoT-module base, not unmodified handsets (COMM-674, COMM-675). [DERIVED]
6. **No verdict.** This doc establishes the device gate, the standardization roadmap, the structural ceiling, and the service-tier ladder. Whether owning Iridium's spectrum, constellation, ground, and ~2.5M customers is worth it, and whether a SEPARATE cellular (lane 2) build is the phone-reaching play, are questions this doc sets up but does not answer. [DERIVED]
