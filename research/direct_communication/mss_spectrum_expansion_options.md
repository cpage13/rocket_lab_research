## Answer First

**1. Is there more L-band? Effectively no.** The L-band MSS allocation usable for satellite-to-terminal service (roughly 1525-1559 MHz down and 1610-1660.5 MHz up, plus the 1670-1675 MHz adjunct) is ENTIRELY held by four parties in the Americas (a fifth, Thuraya/Space42, holds only Regions 1+3, no Americas rights), and none has a realistically-acquirable free block for a new entrant as of 2026-07-01 [FACT/DERIVED]:
- **Iridium** holds 1616-1626.5 MHz (10.5 MHz coordinated, 7.775 MHz exclusive). This is now RKLB's via the acquisition, so it is the baseline you already own, not new spectrum.
- **Globalstar** holds 1610-1618.725 MHz L-band uplink (8.725 MHz total: 7.775 MHz exclusive at 1610-1617.775 plus the 0.95 MHz shared with Iridium at 1617.775-1618.725) plus 2483.5-2500 MHz S-band. It is NOT for sale to RKLB: **Amazon announced its acquisition of Globalstar on April 14, 2026** (~$11.6B, $90.00/share, close expected 2027 pending FCC review), with Apple (a ~20% holder taking 85% of network capacity) consenting in writing and Amazon honoring the Apple arrangement [FACT].
- **Viasat (Inmarsat)** holds the large L-band MSS position at 1525-1559 MHz / 1626.5-1660.5 MHz (globally coordinated, ~34 MHz per direction of allocation). Viasat is an operating incumbent using this spectrum for its core Inmarsat maritime/aviation/government business; it is not offering it for sale, and its North American L-band is already monetized through the 2007 Ligado cooperation agreement (the AST payment stream flows through Ligado to Viasat through 2107). In the AST deal Viasat-Inmarsat appears only as a creditor being repaid, not as a spectrum seller [FACT].
- **Ligado** held ~40 MHz of mid-L-band (1526-1536 / 1627.5-1637.5 / 1646.5-1656.5 MHz) plus access to 5 MHz at 1670-1675 MHz. This is the one block that recently changed hands, and it went to AST SpaceMobile: AST leased up to 45 MHz for 80+ years (through 2107) in the US and Canada, a $550M upfront payment ($535M of it repaying Ligado's debt to Viasat-Inmarsat) plus ~$16M/quarter escalating 3%/yr, consummated through Ligado's January 2025 Chapter 11 bankruptcy (court approval July 1, 2025). AST thereby became the LARGEST US L-band MSS rights-holder (~40 MHz, roughly 4x Iridium's 10.5 MHz coordinated span). After that lease, essentially nothing usable is left unleased at Ligado for a different entrant [FACT].

The only "exceptions" are not purchases, and neither is available today: (a) **regulatory reallocation inside the Big LEO band**: Iridium itself petitioned the FCC on December 23, 2025 to share Globalstar's 1610-1616 MHz and gain broader 1.6 GHz access; Globalstar opposes it, SpaceX has separately sought 1.6 GHz access, and the outcome is open (post-close this contest becomes RKLB versus Amazon); and (b) **1675-1680 MHz**, which is NOT MSS at all: it is the NOAA meteorological downlink band that NTIA formally identified on February 26, 2026 as its first OBBBA repurposing candidate for terrestrial mobile use via a future FCC auction, no date set. So the founder is correct: there is no more L-band to BUY or LEASE. The L-band table is full; the only open paths run through the FCC's rulemaking docket, not the market.

**2. The S-band / 2 GHz MSS door is also nearly shut, for the same reason (bought or committed).** The 2 GHz MSS band (1980-2010 / 2170-2200 MHz, 60 MHz total) breaks down as: 40 MHz of it (the AWS-4 region, 2000-2020 / 2180-2200 MHz) went to SpaceX inside the ~$17B EchoStar deal (approved 2026, GONE); Omnispace's ~60 MHz of globally coordinated 2 GHz S-band (ITU priority filings) is committed into the announced Lynk Global merger (October 2025, SES as strategic shareholder, regulatory approval pending); and the adjacent 2483.5-2500 MHz S-band belongs to Globalstar (Apple-committed, Amazon-acquired, above). There is no clean, unencumbered S-band MSS block for RKLB-Iridium to acquire today either [FACT/DERIVED].

**3. If RKLB-Iridium wants cellular-like reach beyond ~8 MHz, the cellular doors are the SAME two the corpus already established, and neither is L-band.** Door A: lease a carrier's terrestrial cellular band under SCS (near-zero spectrum capex, thin slice ~2x5 MHz, partner-gated and country-by-country). Door B: buy cellular spectrum outright (~$1-2B for ~8 MHz, ~$6-9B for ~25 MHz US, at the ~$0.65-1.03/MHz-POP secondary anchors; ~$32-90B for a 100-200 MHz US+Europe position). These reach unmodified phones; L-band does not. (COMM-488/489/491, COMM-237..246.)

**4. If RKLB-Iridium wants video-grade DATA RATES (not phone reach), the Ku/Ka broadband-to-terminal lane is wide open, and Iridium already holds Ka.** For broadband to a DISH or terminal (not a phone), the satellite bands are abundant (multiple GHz of Ku and Ka), accessed by ITU filing plus FCC market access rather than a multi-billion auction, and Iridium already holds Ka feeder (19.4-19.6 / 29.1-29.3 GHz) and cross-link (~23 GHz) authorizations. This is a DIFFERENT PRODUCT (broadband to terminals, the Starlink lane) from cellular-to-phone; it delivers the data rate but not the unmodified-phone reach. (Cross-ref the three lanes, COMM-634.)

**5. The international asymmetry is the heart of the max-outcome case.** Iridium's L-band is ONE globally-coordinated MSS allocation: it works everywhere on Earth under a single ITU-primary position the US administers. Cellular SCS is the opposite: country-by-country, each national regulator granting its own approval and each requiring a local carrier partner who holds that nation's cellular spectrum (T-Mobile in the US, Rakuten in Japan, VodafoneThree in the UK, Bharti Airtel in India). For a global play, the owned-global L-band is a structural advantage the rented-national cellular slice cannot match; but the L-band reaches only purpose-built terminals, so the two are not substitutes (COMM-633).

**6. The ranked menu (the deliverable).** From most to least realistic for RKLB-Iridium as of 2026-07-01: (1) Ku/Ka broadband to terminals (abundant, accessible, but not phones); (2) cellular SCS lease (thin, partner-gated, reaches phones); (3) improve throughput WITHIN the owned L-band (no new spectrum, engineering only); (4) cellular outright buy (billions, reaches phones); (5) more L-band MSS (essentially unavailable); (6) S-band / 2 GHz MSS (mostly gone or committed). Full table in Section 6.

**Confidence: high on the "no more L-band" verdict** (every L-band MSS holder is enumerated and each is held/committed/sold, multi-source). **High on the Ligado-AST lease terms and consummation** (SpaceNews + DCD + Mobile World Live + Via Satellite + Ligado's own statement + AST SEC 8-Ks, consistent). **High on the Amazon-Globalstar acquisition and the Omnispace-Lynk merger** (Bloomberg/CNBC/SatNews; SpaceNews/Omnispace/Space Intel Report). **High on the cellular doors and the Ka/Ku abundance** (carried from the corpus, multi-source). **High on the international asymmetry** (ITU global MSS allocation vs country-by-country SCS, multi-source). The softest items: the exact Viasat-exclusive residual MHz under the North American coordination (dynamic, [UNKNOWN]) and the EchoStar Mobile Europe remnant (flagged).

## 1. Is There More L-Band? The Complete MSS Inventory (the 1525-1680 MHz Region)

The L-band MSS allocation is a small, crowded, globally-coordinated band. Below is every holder in the satellite-relevant L-band, with MHz held, encumbrance, and acquirability by RKLB-Iridium as of 2026-07-01. The verdict is at the end of the section: the band is full.

### 1.1 The band map

The L-band MSS spectrum used for satellite-to-terminal links sits in two paired sub-ranges (plus small adjuncts):
- **Space-to-Earth (downlink to terminals): ~1525-1559 MHz** (the Inmarsat/Viasat and Ligado downlink region; note GPS/GNSS RNSS occupies 1559-1610 MHz between the two, which is WHY the L-band split exists and why emission limits are tight, Section 1.6).
- **Earth-to-space (uplink from terminals): ~1610-1660.5 MHz** (Globalstar and Iridium at the low end ~1610-1626.5 MHz, the "Big LEO" band; Ligado and Inmarsat/Viasat above ~1626.5-1660.5 MHz).
- **Adjuncts: 1670-1675 MHz** (a 5 MHz band held under the Crown Castle / One Dot Six lease, now subleased to AST as part of the Ligado deal) and **1675-1680 MHz** (NOT MSS: the NOAA meteorological downlink band, now NTIA's first OBBBA repurposing candidate, Section 1.6).

[FACT on the band structure] Sources: [Wikipedia: L band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_band) (GNSS 1559-1610 MHz between the MSS sub-bands; Inmarsat 1525-1559 down / 1626.5-1660.5 up; Iridium 1616-1626.5); [Federal Register: Impact of L-Band MSS Direct-to-Device Operations on GPS](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/27/2024-30760/impact-of-l-band-mss-direct-to-device-operations-on-gps) (L-band MSS user equipment at 1610-1660.5 MHz adjacent to the 1559-1610 MHz RNSS/GPS band).

### 1.2 Iridium (now RKLB): the owned baseline, not new spectrum

Iridium holds **1616-1626.5 MHz (10.5 MHz coordinated span; 7.775 MHz exclusive at 1618.725-1626.5 MHz; 0.95 MHz shared with Globalstar; plus a 500 kHz simplex/ring-alert band at 1626.0-1626.5 MHz)** [FACT, carried from COMM-611]. Via the June 29 2026 acquisition (CONFIRMED, [`../rocket_lab/iridium_acquisition.md`](../rocket_lab/iridium_acquisition.md)), this is now Rocket Lab's. **Acquirability: already owned.** It is the baseline the founder's question starts FROM (the ~8 MHz exclusive), not additional L-band. Source: corpus COMM-611 ([Wikipedia: Iridium satellite constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iridium_satellite_constellation)).

### 1.3 Globalstar: held, and doubly locked (Apple-committed, Amazon-acquired)

Globalstar holds an **L-band uplink at 1610-1618.725 MHz (8.725 MHz total: 7.775 MHz exclusive at 1610-1617.775 MHz, plus the 0.95 MHz at 1617.775-1618.725 MHz shared with Iridium under the FCC's Big LEO band plan)** and an **S-band downlink at 2483.5-2500 MHz (16.5 MHz, of which 11.5 MHz is also authorized terrestrially as 3GPP Band 53/n53)** [FACT] ([FCC DA-26-398, Big LEO band assignments](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-398A1.pdf); [Wikipedia: L band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_band); [SatNews: Amazon to acquire Globalstar, spectrum profile](https://satnews.com/2026/04/14/amazon-to-acquire-globalstar-for-11-6-billion-to-fuel-d2d-expansion/)). Its 31-satellite L-band constellation is what powers Apple's iPhone Emergency SOS.

**Acquirability: no, twice over.** First, Globalstar is committed to Apple: under the November 2024 arrangement Apple made a ~$1.5-1.7B investment (a ~$400M equity stake for ~20%, ~$1.1B in staggered prepayments, plus ~$232M debt refinancing), and Globalstar allocates **85% of its network capacity to Apple** with priority-access rights [FACT] ([CNBC: Apple commits $1.5B to Globalstar](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/11/01/apple-commits-1point5-billion-to-globalstar-for-iphone-satellite-services.html); [SpaceNews: Globalstar soars on Apple investment](https://spacenews.com/globalstar-soars-on-apples-1-5-billion-satellite-investment/); [Via Satellite: Apple invests $1.5B in Globalstar](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2024/11/01/apple-invests-1-5b-in-globalstar-to-fund-a-new-constellation/)). Second, and decisively: **Amazon announced its acquisition of Globalstar on April 14, 2026**, at ~$11.6B ($90.00 per share, cash or 0.32 Amazon shares capped at $90), to fold Globalstar's constellation, ~24 gateways, and MSS licenses (L-band + S-band, spanning 120+ countries) into Project Kuiper's direct-to-device push; close expected **2027, pending FCC review** (Chairman Carr confirmed the FCC will review it). Apple consented in writing, and an Amazon-Apple agreement preserves iPhone Emergency SOS continuity (the 85% capacity arrangement honored) [FACT] ([Bloomberg: Amazon to buy Globalstar for $11.6B](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-14/amazon-to-buy-satellite-operator-globalstar-for-90-a-share-in-cash-or-stock); [CNBC: Amazon to buy Globalstar](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/14/amazon-globalstar-satellite-leo-internet.html); [SatNews: Amazon to acquire Globalstar for $11.6B](https://satnews.com/2026/04/14/amazon-to-acquire-globalstar-for-11-6-billion-to-fuel-d2d-expansion/); [Forbes: Amazon acquires Globalstar](https://www.forbes.com/sites/aliciapark/2026/04/14/amazon-buys-satellite-firm-for-116-billion-as-it-catches-up-with-spacexs-starlink/)). Globalstar's L-band and S-band are not available to RKLB-Iridium; post-close, the neighbor on the 1.6 GHz dial is Amazon.

**The live regulatory contest inside the Big LEO band (the one non-purchase path to "more L-band").** On December 23, 2025, Iridium petitioned the FCC for rulemaking to expand its 1.6 GHz access: to share the 1610-1616 MHz uplink spectrum authorized to Globalstar (Iridium says it can share without harmful interference) while retaining its own band. Globalstar urged denial, defending the 2007 order that rebalanced the Big LEO band into exclusive operating rights; SpaceX has separately and repeatedly sought 1.6 GHz access (which Iridium and Globalstar jointly opposed) [FACT] ([Communications Daily: Fight brewing over 1.6 GHz as Iridium petitions FCC](https://communicationsdaily.com/news/2025/12/30/fight-seen-brewing-over-16-ghz-as-iridium-petitions-fcc-for-greater-access-2512290021); [Broadband Breakfast: Globalstar opposed to other satellite operators in 1.6 GHz](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/globalstar-opposed-to-other-satellite-operators-in-1-6-ghz/); [Fierce Network: Globalstar not interested in Iridium's sharing proposal](https://www.fierce-network.com/tech/globalstar-not-interested-iridium-s-latest-spectrum-sharing-proposal)). If granted, this would add usable 1.6 GHz MHz to the Iridium (RKLB) system WITHOUT a purchase; it is contested, unscheduled, and post-close it becomes an RKLB-versus-Amazon proceeding. This petition, not any acquisition, is the one live avenue to more L-band.

### 1.4 Viasat (Inmarsat): the large L-band block, held by an operating incumbent

Inmarsat (acquired by Viasat in 2023) holds the largest L-band MSS position: **1525-1559 MHz (downlink) and 1626.5-1660.5 MHz (uplink)**, a globally-coordinated MSS allocation of ~34 MHz per direction used for its core maritime, aviation-safety, and government business (Inmarsat is the safety-of-life GMDSS provider) [FACT] ([Wikipedia: L band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_band), Inmarsat 1525-1559 / 1626.5-1660.5 MHz; [Wikipedia: Inmarsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inmarsat)).

**The North American structure matters:** over North America, this band is coordinated between Inmarsat and Ligado under a **2007 L-band cooperation agreement** that rearranges the two holders' assignments into contiguous swaths (Ligado's usable ~40 MHz exists BECAUSE Inmarsat cooperates for payment), which is exactly why Ligado's largest creditor was Viasat-Inmarsat and why the AST money flows through Ligado to Viasat ($535M plus ~$16M/quarter escalating 3%/yr to Inmarsat through 2107) [FACT] ([SpaceNews: AST reaches deal to bankroll Ligado's Viasat settlement](https://spacenews.com/ast-spacemobile-reaches-deal-to-bankroll-ligados-viasat-settlement/); [Via Satellite: Viasat, Ligado, and AST reach spectrum deal in bankruptcy case](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2025/06/16/viasat-ligado-and-ast-spacemobile-reach-spectrum-deal-in-bankruptcy-case/); [Viasat: comprehensive agreement with Ligado](https://www.viasat.com/news/latest-news/corporate/2025/viasat-announces-comprehensive-agreement-with-ligado-networks/)). The exact Viasat-exclusive residual MHz under that dynamic coordination is [UNKNOWN] (it is not a fixed public number).

**Acquirability: no.** This is a working incumbent's operating spectrum, not a distressed or idle block, and Viasat is using it; its North American L-band value is furthermore already contracted into the Ligado-AST chain through 2107. Tellingly, in the AST-Ligado deal Viasat-Inmarsat appears only as a CREDITOR being repaid ($535M of the $550M went to pay down Ligado's debt to Viasat-Inmarsat) and as a party providing regulatory support for AST, NOT as a seller of its OWN L-band [FACT] ([SpaceNews: AST strikes spectrum deal amid Ligado bankruptcy](https://spacenews.com/ast-spacemobile-strikes-spectrum-deal-amid-ligado-networks-bankruptcy/); [DCD: AST pays $550M for North America spectrum](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ast-spacemobile-pays-550m-to-access-premium-spectrum-in-north-america-for-satellite-d2d/)). Viasat's Inmarsat L-band is not on the market.

### 1.5 Ligado to AST: the one block that moved, and it is now spoken-for

Ligado Networks held the classic disputed mid-L-band: **~40 MHz at 1526-1536 MHz, 1627.5-1637.5 MHz, and 1646.5-1656.5 MHz** (the bands the FCC authorized for a low-power terrestrial network in FCC Order 20-48, April 2020, over DoD/GPS objections), plus **5 MHz at 1670-1675 MHz** [FACT] ([FCC 20-48](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-48A1.pdf), the three authorized bands; [EveryCRSReport: Ligado, the L-band, and GPS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF11558.html)).

**This is the block that recently changed hands, and it went to AST SpaceMobile, not to the open market:**
- **AST leased up to 45 MHz** (up to 40 MHz of Ligado's L-band MSS + up to 5 MHz at 1670-1675 MHz) for **more than 80 years** (through 2107), covering the **US and Canada**, for direct-to-device [FACT] ([Mobile World Live: AST, Ligado agree spectrum deal](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/north-america/ast-ligado-agree-spectrum-deal/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ast-spacemobile-pays-550m-to-access-premium-spectrum-in-north-america-for-satellite-d2d/); [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/ast-spacemobile-strikes-spectrum-deal-amid-ligado-networks-bankruptcy/)).
- **Terms: $550M upfront** (of which **$535M repays Ligado's debt to Viasat-Inmarsat**), warrants convertible into AST shares (~$120M value), plus ongoing lease payments starting **~$16M/quarter from September 30, 2025, escalating 3%/yr through 2107** [FACT] (DCD + SpaceNews + Mobile World Live, above; [AST SpaceMobile 8-K, FY2025, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001780312/000149315225011675/ex99-1.htm)).
- **Consummated through Ligado's Chapter 11 bankruptcy (filed January 5, 2025):** the restructuring converted ~$7.8B of debt to equity and reduced future debt to ~$1.2B; the **bankruptcy court approved the AST transaction on July 1, 2025** and Ligado confirmed it publicly [FACT] ([Ligado: statement on bankruptcy court approval of AST SpaceMobile transaction](https://ligado.com/press/ligado-networks-statement-on-bankruptcy-court-approval-of-ast-spacemobile-transaction/); [Via Satellite: Viasat, Ligado, and AST reach spectrum deal in bankruptcy case, June 16 2025](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2025/06/16/viasat-ligado-and-ast-spacemobile-reach-spectrum-deal-in-bankruptcy-case/); [SatNews: AST settlement term sheet for up to 45 MHz](https://news.satnews.com/2025/06/15/ast-spacemobile-announces-settlement-term-sheet-facilitating-long-term-access-to-up-to-45-mhz-of-premium-lower-mid-band-spectrum-in-north-america-for-d2d-apps/); [Inside Towers: Ligado files Chapter 11, inks AST deal](https://insidetowers.com/ligado-networks-files-chapter-11-inks-spectrum-deal-with-ast-spacemobile/)).

After the AST lease, there is essentially no usable Ligado L-band left unleased for a different entrant** [DERIVED from the "up to 45 MHz" scope covering Ligado's usable holdings]. The block that was the last realistically-transferable L-band in North America is now AST's for 80+ years, and with it **AST holds the largest US L-band MSS position (~40 MHz), roughly four times Iridium's 10.5 MHz coordinated span** [DERIVED from COMM-692/687]. **This is the single most decisive datapoint behind the "no more L-band" verdict.**

### 1.6 The other L-band items, and why they are not doors

- **Thuraya (Space42): regional, operating, and not for sale.** Thuraya, now part of the UAE's Space42 (the Yahsat-Bayanat merger), holds L-band MSS coordinated for ITU Regions 1 and 3 (Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Asia), NOT the Americas: its just-launched Thuraya-4 satellite (Airbus-built, launched January 2025, commercial service from November 2025) serves customers in 100+ countries across exactly that footprint [FACT] ([Via Satellite: Space42's Thuraya-4 enters service](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2025/11/05/space42s-thuraya-4-satellite-enters-service-now-available-to-global-customers/); [GlobeNewswire/Space42: Thuraya-4 global availability](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/11/05/3181143/0/en/Space42-Announces-Global-Availability-of-Thuraya-4-Its-Next-Generation-Communications-Satellite-System.html)). It has no North American L-band coordination rights (it is not a party to the North American L-band coordination framework among Inmarsat/Ligado and the region's MSS operators) [FACT-SS on the coordination exclusion] ([SkyTerra/MSV 8-K era filings on the North American L-band cooperation structure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000756502/000114420407068694/v097951_ex99-1.htm)), and it is a state-backed operator that just invested in a new satellite: an operating incumbent on the wrong side of the map, not a seller.
- **1670-1675 MHz** (5 MHz adjunct): already counted inside the AST arrangement (the Crown Castle / One Dot Six sublease, Section 1.5); it carries meteorological incumbency that limits clean use; not a free door [FACT] (AST-Ligado terms, above).
- **1675-1680 MHz is NOT MSS, and it is heading to a terrestrial auction someday.** This 5 MHz is the NOAA meteorological-satellite downlink band (GOES data broadcast). On **February 26, 2026, NTIA formally notified the FCC that it has identified 1675-1680 MHz as its FIRST band repurposed under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) spectrum pipeline** (the statute requiring 500 MHz of federal spectrum for full-power commercial licensed use), pointed at mobile-carrier use, contingent on an Alternate Distribution System for the NOAA data; NTIA is also studying the adjacent **1680-1695 MHz for mobile and direct-to-device use**. No auction date is set, and the weather community contests it [FACT] ([NTIA letter to FCC Chairman Carr, Feb 26 2026](https://www.ntia.gov/sites/default/files/2026-03/1675-mhz-identification-letter.pdf); [Broadband Breakfast: NTIA officially taps 5 MHz of NOAA spectrum](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ntia-officially-taps-5-megahertz-of-noaa-spectrum-for-wireless-use/); [Hogan Lovells: America's new commercial spectrum pipeline](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/americas-new-commercial-spectrum-pipeline-part-1)). If auctioned, it would be TERRESTRIAL flexible-use spectrum (with a possible D2D angle at 1680-1695), not an MSS allocation: a future auction ticket anyone could bid on, not L-band MSS available today.
- **The GPS-interference constraint on the whole band (why L-band is hard to push).** Because GPS/GNSS (RNSS) sits at 1559-1610 MHz between the two MSS sub-bands, terrestrial or high-power L-band use carries tight out-of-band-emission limits: the FCC 20-48 order caps Ligado ATC base-station OOBE at, for example, -85 dBW/MHz in 1610-1650 MHz and -100 dBW/MHz in the 1559-1610 MHz GPS band [FACT] ([FCC 20-48](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-20-48A1.pdf); [Federal Register: L-Band MSS D2D impact on GPS](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/27/2024-30760/impact-of-l-band-mss-direct-to-device-operations-on-gps)). This is a reason L-band is not a simple lever to crank up: even the holder of the spectrum is emission-constrained by the GPS neighbor. It does not change the availability answer, but it bears on the "can you just get more data rate out of L-band" question (Section 6, option 3).

### 1.7 Verdict on L-band: the band is full

Summing the inventory: Iridium (yours, 10.5 MHz coordinated), Globalstar (8.725 MHz L + 16.5 MHz S, Apple-committed and Amazon-acquired), Viasat-Inmarsat (~34+34 MHz allocation, operating incumbent, its North American value contracted into the Ligado-AST chain through 2107), Ligado (~40 MHz + the 1670-1675 sublease, now AST's for 80+ years, making AST the largest US L-band MSS holder), Thuraya/Space42 (Regions 1+3 only, no Americas rights, operating and state-backed). Every usable slice of the L-band MSS allocation is held, and each holder is either you, acquired by a hyperscaler, an operating incumbent, or already leased away for generations. **There is no more L-band MSS realistically available for RKLB-Iridium to BUY or LEASE as of 2026-07-01.** The founder's expectation is confirmed, with zero greenfield L-band anywhere. The only paths that could ever add L-band MHz are regulatory, not commercial: (a) Iridium's own pending FCC petition (Dec 23, 2025) to share Globalstar's 1610-1616 MHz, contested and unscheduled (Section 1.3); and (b) the someday-auction of 1675-1680 MHz (and possibly 1680-1695 MHz), which is NOT MSS and has no date (Section 1.6).

## 2. The S-Band and Other MSS Options (2 GHz MSS)

The natural next question after "no more L-band" is "what about the S-band MSS one octave up?" The answer is nearly the same: mostly gone (bought by SpaceX), committed (Apple/Amazon), or merging (Omnispace into Lynk, with SES backing). Every S-band MSS block has a name on it.

### 2.1 The 2 GHz MSS band map (60 MHz total)

The 2 GHz MSS band is **1980-2010 MHz (uplink) / 2170-2200 MHz (downlink), 30 MHz each way, 60 MHz total**, allocated to MSS worldwide by the ITU [FACT] ([ITU Radio Regulations; Wikipedia: Mobile satellite service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_satellite_service)). In the US, part of this band was repurposed as terrestrial-flexible AWS-4.

### 2.2 EchoStar AWS-4 (the 2 GHz MSS core): GONE to SpaceX

The AWS-4 band (**2000-2020 MHz up / 2180-2200 MHz down, 40 MHz**) is the 2 GHz MSS band as held by DISH/EchoStar (inherited via the TerreStar and ICO/DBSD MSS lineage). This went to SpaceX inside the ~$17B EchoStar deal (approved 2026), alongside the H-block (10 MHz at ~1.9 GHz) and AWS-3 (15 MHz), for the ~65 MHz total [FACT, carried from COMM-186/489] ([Bloomberg Law: FCC grants SpaceX ~65MHz](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/fcc-grants-spacex-65mhz-of-midband-spectrum-for-d2d-network); [DCD: SpaceX acquires EchoStar AWS-4/H-block $17B](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/spacex-acquires-echostars-aws-4-and-h-block-spectrum-for-17bn/)). **Disposition: 40 MHz of the 60 MHz 2 GHz band is SpaceX-owned, gone.** The TerreStar -> DISH -> EchoStar -> SpaceX lineage means this block is fully accounted for and off the market.

### 2.3 Omnispace: the 2 GHz S-band holder, now merging into Lynk with SES behind it

Omnispace holds **~60 MHz of globally coordinated 2 GHz S-band** (the 1980-2010 / 2170-2200 MHz band, via high-priority ITU filings inherited from the ICO/New ICO lineage) and has pursued a global S-band NGSO direct-to-device position (it demonstrated 5G NB-IoT NTN over its S-band NGSO satellite with Gatehouse Satcom and Nordic Semiconductor in May 2025) [FACT] ([SpaceNews: Lynk Global and Omnispace to merge in race for D2D spectrum](https://spacenews.com/lynk-global-and-omnispace-to-merge-in-race-for-direct-to-device-satellite-spectrum/); [Omnispace: Lynk and Omnispace announce plans to merge](https://omnispace.com/lynk-and-omnispace-announce-plans-to-merge-to-deliver/); [PR Newswire: Omnispace and Lacuna NGSO IoT demo](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/omnispace-and-lacuna-showcase-breakthrough-ngso-iot-satellite-connectivity-301938096.html)).

**Status and acquirability: spoken-for.** In **October 2025 Lynk Global and Omnispace announced plans to merge**, explicitly to combine Lynk's constellation with "Omnispace's 60 MHz of globally coordinated S-band spectrum and its high-priority ITU filings," with **SES becoming a major strategic shareholder** of the combined company (SES also supplies the ground segment); regulatory approval of the merger was still pending as of mid-2026, with Lynk targeting service from a 640-satellite constellation in 2027 [FACT] ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/lynk-global-and-omnispace-to-merge-in-race-for-direct-to-device-satellite-spectrum/); [Omnispace announcement](https://omnispace.com/lynk-and-omnispace-announce-plans-to-merge-to-deliver/); [Space Intel Report: Lynk 640-satellite constellation, Omnispace merger approval pending](https://www.spaceintelreport.com/lynk-global-our-640-satellite-constellation-to-start-service-in-2027-regulatory-ok-of-omnispace-merger-still-pending/)). The last unattached 2 GHz S-band MSS position is therefore committed into the Lynk-SES combination, not available to RKLB-Iridium; whether the merged entity would ever sell is [UNKNOWN] and speculative.

### 2.4 Globalstar S-band 2483.5-2500 MHz: Apple-committed, Amazon-acquired

Globalstar's S-band downlink at **2483.5-2500 MHz (16.5 MHz)**, including its terrestrial Band 53/n53 authority (2483.5-2495 MHz, 11.5 MHz TDD), is part of the Apple-committed asset base and rides inside the announced Amazon acquisition (Section 1.3): 85% capacity to Apple, ~20% Apple-owned, Amazon deal announced April 14, 2026, close expected 2027 [FACT] (sources in Section 1.3). **Disposition: committed and acquired, not acquirable.**

### 2.5 EchoStar Mobile (Europe) and remnants

EchoStar Mobile holds a pan-European 2 GHz MSS authorization (the European S-band MSS award lineage, originally Solaris Mobile); its current disposition after the US EchoStar-SpaceX deal is a named [UNKNOWN] here: a European-scope question, not a US block, and not verified in this pass. Any 2020-2025 MHz adjunct holding is likewise [UNKNOWN].

### 2.6 Verdict on S-band / 2 GHz MSS

Of the 60 MHz 2 GHz MSS band: **40 MHz (AWS-4) is SpaceX-owned (gone)**; the Omnispace ~60 MHz globally-coordinated ITU position is **merging into Lynk-SES** (announced October 2025, approval pending); and the adjacent Globalstar 16.5 MHz S-band is **Apple-committed and inside the Amazon acquisition**. There is no clean, unencumbered, priced S-band MSS block for RKLB-Iridium to acquire today. The S-band door is as shut as the L-band door, for the same reason: every block that could move has already moved (to SpaceX), been committed (to Apple, then Amazon), or paired off (Omnispace-Lynk-SES). The would-be sellers all found their buyers in 2025-2026.

## 3. The Cellular Doors, Restated (Not L-Band, Reaches Phones)

If the goal shifts from "more MSS spectrum" to "reach unmodified phones like a cellular network," the corpus already owns the answer: two doors, neither in L-band, both cellular. Restated in one paragraph each, cited to the corpus (not re-derived).

**Door A: SCS lease from a carrier (near-zero capex, thin, partner-gated, country-by-country).** Under the FCC's Supplemental Coverage from Space rules, a satellite operator rides a terrestrial carrier's licensed cellular band as a gap-filler, on a secondary basis, only where the carrier partner holds all the licenses and leases them before grant. Spectrum capex is near zero (a commercial fee, e.g. AST/Verizon ~$100M, not a license purchase), but the slice is thin (Starlink rides T-Mobile's PCS G-block, 2x5 MHz) and you need a willing carrier partner in each country. This is the realistic-entrant door, and it reaches unmodified phones because it uses cellular bands the phone already supports (COMM-488) [FACT, corpus] ([`dtc_spectrum_access.md`](dtc_spectrum_access.md), COMM-488).

**Door B: outright cellular purchase (billions, reaches phones, distressed-MSS-only supply).** Buying dedicated cellular spectrum costs roughly auction prices on the secondary market: at the ~$0.65-1.03/MHz-POP US anchors, ~8 MHz US is on the order of ~$1-2B and ~25 MHz US is on the order of ~$6-9B (DERIVED at ~342M POPs), and a competitive US+Europe position (100-200 MHz) is ~$32-90B, spectrum-only. The only buyable blocks are distressed MSS/satellite holdings (the EchoStar precedent: ~$17B for ~65 MHz), and SpaceX just took the largest piece. This owns dedicated spectrum that reaches phones, but at a hyperscaler's price (COMM-489, COMM-491, COMM-237..246) [FACT/DERIVED, corpus] ([`dtc_spectrum_access.md`](dtc_spectrum_access.md), COMM-489/491; [`spectrum_purchase_and_6g.md`](spectrum_purchase_and_6g.md), COMM-237..246).

**The load-bearing point (COMM-633): neither door is L-band, and L-band cannot substitute.** Owning Iridium's L-band does not give RKLB-Iridium either door, because a phone has cellular radios and no L-band MSS radio. The cellular reach is a separate acquisition from the MSS spectrum RKLB already owns.

## 4. The Ku/Ka Broadband Lane (Video-Grade to Terminals, Not Phones)

If the goal is video-grade DATA RATE rather than phone reach, the answer is the broadband lane, and it is the most open door of all, because for a DISH or terminal (not a bare phone) the wide satellite bands are abundant. Ku (~10.7-12.7 GHz down / 14-14.5 GHz up) and Ka (~17.8-20.2 GHz down / 27.5-30 GHz up) offer multiple GHz of bandwidth, and NGSO systems coordinate and share (ITU filing plus FCC market access, the SpaceX/OneWeb/Kuiper/Telesat path) rather than buying an exclusive block at a multi-billion auction (COMM-178..190) [FACT, corpus + broadband-lane research]. A modern NGSO broadband satellite uses on the order of ~2 GHz of Ku user downlink plus multi-GHz Ka feeder (GHz, not MHz), two to three orders of magnitude more raw bandwidth than the ~8 MHz L-band, precisely because the dish supplies gain and can point (the aperture-and-spectrum asymmetry, COMM-313). Critically, **Iridium already holds Ka feeder (19.4-19.6 / 29.1-29.3 GHz) and cross-link (~23 GHz) authorizations** (COMM-612), so RKLB-Iridium already has a Ka foothold. But this is the BROADBAND product lane to terminals, a DIFFERENT product from cellular-to-phone: Ku/Ka delivers the video-grade data rate to a dish, and never to an unmodified handset, which has no Ku/Ka radio. Held apart from the cellular and MSS lanes, this is the clean answer to "how do we get video-grade rates": build broadband to terminals on abundant Ku/Ka, not on L-band (cross-ref the three lanes, COMM-634).

## 5. The International Dimension (Global L-Band vs Country-by-Country Cellular)

This section is the heart of the max-outcome case, because it is where the owned L-band asset is structurally different from, and in one dimension superior to, the cellular alternatives.

**Iridium's L-band is ONE globally-coordinated allocation.** It is an ITU-primary MSS position that the United States administers on Iridium's behalf, and it works everywhere on Earth under that single coordination: one allocation, one waveform, global reach including oceans and poles [FACT] ([Wikipedia: Mobile satellite service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_satellite_service); [FCC DA-01-1636, Iridium global operation under US licensing per ITU](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-01-1636A1.pdf); corpus COMM-611/633). A satellite phone or IoT terminal that works on Iridium in the US works on Iridium in the middle of the Pacific and in Antarctica, with no per-country spectrum deal. This is exactly why the deal materials call it "rare": globally-harmonized MSS spectrum is scarce and cannot be assembled country-by-country after the fact.

**Cellular SCS is the opposite: country-by-country, partner-by-partner.** Direct-to-cell over a carrier's terrestrial band requires, in EACH country, (a) that nation's regulator to approve the SCS/direct-to-device operation, and (b) a local carrier partner who holds that nation's cellular spectrum and agrees to share it. The pattern is visible in who the operators partnered with: Starlink with T-Mobile (US), KDDI/Docomo/SoftBank (Japan), Virgin Media O2 (UK); AST with AT&T/Verizon (US), Rakuten (Japan), VodafoneThree (UK), Bharti Airtel (India), plus Vodafone as a global investor-partner. Each is a separate national approval on separate national spectrum [FACT] ([SpaceNews: FCC clears AST constellation, international partners](https://spacenews.com/fcc-clears-ast-spacemobile-constellation-as-launch-setback-clouds-ramp-up/); [Broadband Breakfast: FCC grants AST direct-to-cell](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/fcc-grants-ast-spacemobiles-direct-to-cell-request/)). Globally, direct-to-cell "requires coordination through the ITU, and each country must grant its own regulatory approval," and each deployment rides spectrum "licensed to terrestrial carriers" in that country [FACT] (same sources).

**What this means for a global max-outcome play.** The owned global L-band is a single asset that already spans the planet, with no partner to negotiate and no per-country spectrum to assemble; that is a real structural advantage for a global operator, and it is the asset RKLB is buying. But the same L-band reaches only purpose-built terminals, so it does not deliver the mass-market unmodified-phone reach that the country-by-country cellular route does. The asymmetry is the whole trade: owned-and-global-but-terminals-only (L-band MSS) versus rented-and-national-but-reaches-every-phone (cellular SCS). They are complements on different products, not substitutes. Neither the global L-band nor a stack of national cellular deals is, by itself, the whole answer for a global max-outcome case (COMM-633/634).
