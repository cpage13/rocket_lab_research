# Rocket Lab USA — Baseline Financials (FY2020–Q1 2026)

*Research date: May 2026. Prepared for the Rocket Lab orbital AI-inference data center feasibility project.*

> **Purpose:** Establish a meticulously sourced baseline of Rocket Lab's **actual reported financials** — revenue, segments, margins, profitability, cash, backlog, and Electron launch cadence — as the factual foundation for a company-valuation model. This document makes no argument and forecasts nothing; it records what Rocket Lab has reported.

> **Subject:** Rocket Lab Corporation (formerly Rocket Lab USA, Inc.; legal name changed May 2025). Ticker **RKLB**, Nasdaq. SEC CIK 0001819994. Fiscal year ends December 31. The company went public via SPAC merger with Vector Acquisition Corp. on August 25, 2021.

> **Reading guide:** Every hard number carries an inline citation. **[FILING]** = SEC 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K (audited or as-reported). **[PR]** = Rocket Lab earnings press release (filed as an SEC 8-K exhibit — also primary). **[DERIVED]** = our own arithmetic (growth rates, CAGR, margin ratios) computed from filed figures. Headline annual revenue is cross-checked against both an SEC filing and a reputable secondary source.

> **Currency / units:** All figures in US dollars. Rocket Lab reports in USD. Amounts stated "in thousands" in filings are presented here in millions for readability; exact thousands are given where precision matters.

## Summary

- **Revenue trajectory:** Rocket Lab grew total revenue from **$35.2M (FY2020)** to **$601.8M (FY2025)** — a roughly **17x increase over five years**, a **~76.5% revenue CAGR (FY2020→FY2025)**. [FILING; DERIVED] Growth has been uneven: +77% (2021), +239% (2022, the year space-systems acquisitions consolidated), +16% (2023, a digestion year), +78% (2024), +38% (2025). FY2025 was a record year. [FILING]
- **Segment mix flipped, then held:** Rocket Lab reports two segments — **Launch Services** (Electron / HASTE launch) and **Space Systems** (spacecraft manufacturing, components, software, space data). Launch was ~94% of revenue in 2020; after the 2021–2022 acquisitions (Sinclair, Planetary Systems, SolAero, plus the in-house Photon platform) **Space Systems became the majority** — ~67–71% of revenue every year from 2022 through 2025. In FY2025, Space Systems was **$402.8M (66.9%)** and Launch Services **$199.0M (33.1%)**. [FILING]
- **Margins are improving but the company is not profitable.** GAAP gross margin rose every year: **-33.6% (2020) → 9.0% (2022) → 21.0% (2023) → 26.6% (2024) → 34.4% (2025)**. [FILING; DERIVED] Non-GAAP (adjusted) gross margin reached **39.7% in FY2025**. [PR] But Rocket Lab has **never reported a profit**: GAAP net loss every year, widening from **-$55.0M (2020)** to **-$198.2M (2025)**; Adjusted EBITDA also negative every year (-$101.2M in FY2025). [FILING; PR]
- **Balance sheet is well-capitalized.** Cash & equivalents were **$828.7M** at Dec 31, 2025 ($1.10B including marketable securities), rising to **>$2B of total liquidity** after Q1 2026 capital raises. [FILING; PR] Total contracted **backlog was $1.85B at year-end 2025** (73% YoY growth) and **$2.2B after Q1 2026**. [FILING; PR]
- **Operational track record:** Electron has flown reliably. Orbital launches per year: 7 (2020), 6 (2021), 9 (2022), then a step-change to ~10 (2023), 16 (2024) and 21 launch missions (2025, including HASTE). Lifetime Electron record through end-2025 is strong — the only failures in the window were one each in 2020, 2021 and 2023; 2022, 2024 and 2025 were 100%-success years. [FILING; secondary]

**Confidence: High.** All headline figures are drawn from Rocket Lab's SEC filings (10-K) and SEC-filed earnings press releases (8-K exhibits), and the income-statement figures are independently confirmed against the SEC's structured XBRL company-facts database. The one genuinely soft area is per-year Electron launch *counts* before 2023 (sources differ on whether to count by calendar year, by orbital vs. suborbital, and on aborted attempts) — addressed explicitly in §6 and Open Questions. Segment-level *gross profit* is disclosed; segment-level *operating income, EBITDA and assets* are **not** disclosed by the company.

**Reading the segment story:**
- **2020:** Rocket Lab was essentially a pure-play small launch company; Space Systems ($2.1M) was nascent.
- **2021–2022:** The Space Systems segment exploded — from $23M to $150M — driven by acquisitions (Sinclair Interplanetary, Planetary Systems Corp., SolAero) closing in Q4 2021 / Q1 2022 and by the MDA spacecraft-bus contract. The FY2022 10-K attributes ~$109.1M of the 2022 Space Systems increase to acquisitions and ~$18.0M to organic growth. [FILING]
- **2022–2024:** Space Systems held steady at ~71% of revenue; both segments grew. FY2024 Space Systems revenue $310.8M (+80% YoY) was driven by spacecraft manufacturing (MDA and the Space Development Agency programs); FY2024 Launch Services $125.4M (+74% YoY) on 16 launch missions vs. 10 in 2023. [FILING]
- **2025:** Launch Services grew faster (+59%, to $199.0M, on a higher launch cadence and higher revenue per launch) than Space Systems (+30%, to $402.8M), nudging the mix back toward launch (33%/67%). [FILING]

**Revenue by recognition model (FY2025, from the 10-K disaggregated-revenue note):** Point-in-time $266.7M, over-time $335.1M. [FILING] **By product vs. service (FY2025):** Products $371.6M (all Space Systems), Services $230.2M (all $199.0M of Launch Services plus $31.1M of Space Systems services). [FILING]

## 3. Gross Margin — GAAP and Non-GAAP, Total and by Segment

### 3a. Total gross profit and gross margin

GAAP gross profit is from the audited income statements (10-K) and confirmed against SEC XBRL (`GrossProfit`). Non-GAAP (adjusted) gross profit/margin — which excludes stock-based compensation, amortization of acquired intangibles, and certain non-recurring items — is from Rocket Lab's earnings press releases (filed as 8-K exhibits). GAAP gross margin % is **[DERIVED]** = gross profit ÷ revenue.

| Fiscal year | GAAP gross profit | GAAP gross margin | Non-GAAP gross profit | Non-GAAP gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2020 | -$11.817M | **-33.6%** | not separately disclosed | n/a |
| FY2021 | -$1.893M | **-3.0%** | not separately disclosed | n/a |
| FY2022 | $18.990M | **9.0%** | $44.794M | **21.2%** |
| FY2023 | $51.409M | **21.0%** | $68.849M | **28.1%** |

Sources: GAAP gross profit — FY2022 10-K, FY2023 10-K, FY2025 10-K, all confirmed in SEC XBRL [FILING]. Non-GAAP gross profit/margin — FY2023 earnings press release (8-K exhibit 99.1, for FY2022 and FY2023), FY2024 earnings press release (8-K exhibit 99.1, for FY2024), FY2025 earnings press release (for FY2025) [PR]. FY2020–FY2021 non-GAAP gross margin is not broken out in the materials reviewed.

**Trend:** A clear, monotonic margin-expansion story — GAAP gross margin improved by roughly 68 percentage points over five years, from deeply negative to +34.4%. Management attributes the improvement to manufacturing scale, higher launch cadence (lower cost per launch), and mix. The gap between GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin (~5 pts in FY2025) is mostly stock-based compensation and amortization of acquisition-related intangibles.

### 3b. Gross profit by segment

The FY2025 10-K segment note discloses **revenue, cost of revenue, and gross profit by segment** for FY2023–FY2025; the FY2022 10-K does the same for FY2020–FY2022. This is GAAP segment gross profit (the company does **not** publish *non-GAAP* gross margin by segment).

| Fiscal year | Launch Services gross profit (margin) | Space Systems gross profit (margin) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2020 | -$12.787M (**-38.6%**) | $0.970M (**46.7%**) | FY2022 10-K [FILING; DERIVED] |
| FY2021 | -$14.856M (**-38.1%**) | $12.963M (**55.7%**) | FY2022 10-K [FILING; DERIVED] |
| FY2022 | -$6.954M (**-11.5%**) | $25.944M (**17.3%**) | FY2022 10-K [FILING; DERIVED] |
| FY2023 | $8.067M (**11.2%**) | $43.342M (**25.1%**) | FY2025 10-K [FILING; DERIVED] |
| FY2024 | $34.590M (**27.6%**) | $81.559M (**26.2%**) | FY2025 10-K [FILING; DERIVED] |
| FY2025 | $81.270M (**40.8%**) | $125.911M (**31.3%**) | FY2025 10-K [FILING; DERIVED] |

**Reading the segment margins:**
- **Launch Services** ran at a *gross loss* through FY2022 — the small-launch business did not cover its own cost of revenue while cadence was low — then turned positive in 2023 and improved sharply to a **40.8% gross margin in FY2025** as cadence and revenue-per-launch rose and fixed costs were spread over more flights.
- **Space Systems** has been gross-profit-positive every year. Its margin compressed from the mid-50s% in 2020–2021 (when it was a tiny, high-margin components business) into the 17–31% range once large, lower-margin spacecraft-manufacturing contracts (MDA, SDA) dominated the mix.
- Notably, by FY2025 **Launch Services gross margin (40.8%) exceeded Space Systems (31.3%)** — a reversal from earlier years.

> **Important limitation:** Rocket Lab states in the 10-K that management "does not regularly review either reporting segment's total assets or operating expenses," because long-lived assets and facilities are shared. There is therefore **no disclosed segment-level operating income, segment EBITDA, or segment asset base.** Any segment profitability below the gross-profit line must be modeled, not sourced.

## 4. Operating Loss, Net Loss, and Adjusted EBITDA

All GAAP figures from the audited income statements (10-K), confirmed against SEC XBRL (`OperatingIncomeLoss`, `NetIncomeLoss`). Adjusted EBITDA — a non-GAAP measure defined by Rocket Lab as earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, further excluding items management deems unrepresentative of ongoing operations (notably stock-based compensation) — is from the reconciliation tables in the earnings press releases.

| Fiscal year | GAAP operating loss | GAAP net loss | Adjusted EBITDA | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2020 | -$54.952M | -$55.005M | not disclosed in materials reviewed | FY2022 10-K; SEC XBRL [FILING] |
| FY2021 | -$102.053M | -$117.320M | not disclosed in materials reviewed | FY2022 10-K; SEC XBRL [FILING] |
| FY2022 | -$135.204M | -$135.944M | **-$38.525M** | FY2022 10-K; FY2023 PR [FILING; PR] |
| FY2023 | -$177.918M | -$182.571M | **-$90.857M** | FY2023 10-K; FY2023 PR; SEC XBRL [FILING; PR] |
| FY2024 | -$189.801M | -$190.175M | **-$96.943M** | FY2024 10-K; FY2024 PR; SEC XBRL [FILING; PR] |
| FY2025 | -$228.838M | -$198.209M | **-$101.2M** | FY2025 10-K; FY2025 PR; SEC XBRL [FILING; PR] |

**Reading the profitability picture:**
- Rocket Lab has **posted a GAAP net loss in every year of its public history**, and the loss has *widened* in absolute dollars each year even as revenue and gross margin improved — because operating expenses (R&D, particularly the Neutron launch-vehicle development program, plus SG&A) have grown alongside the business.
- The FY2025 10-K explicitly states the company "expect[s] to continue to incur net losses for at least the next 12 months."
- **Note the FY2025 divergence:** GAAP operating loss ($228.8M) is *larger* than GAAP net loss ($198.2M). Net loss is smaller because of net non-operating items below the operating line (interest income on the large cash balance, and other items). In prior years operating loss and net loss tracked closely; in 2025 they separate by ~$31M.
- **Adjusted EBITDA loss has been relatively stable at roughly -$90M to -$101M for FY2023–FY2025** even as revenue grew from $245M to $602M — i.e., incremental revenue is roughly covering incremental cash operating cost, with the EBITDA loss held roughly flat rather than scaling with revenue. The gap between Adjusted EBITDA (-$101.2M) and GAAP net loss (-$198.2M) in FY2025 is primarily stock-based compensation, depreciation & amortization, and interest.

## 6. Electron Launch Cadence and Operational Track Record

Electron is Rocket Lab's small orbital launch vehicle (first orbital launch 2018). HASTE (Hypersonic Accelerator Suborbital Test Electron) is a suborbital derivative introduced in 2023. The medium-lift **Neutron** rocket was still in development and had **not flown** as of Q1 2026.

**Per-year launch counts** — note the sourcing nuance below the table:

| Year | Orbital Electron launches (attempted) | Successful | Failed | "Launch missions" per 10-K (incl. HASTE) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 7 | 6 | 1 | — | FY2022 10-K ("launched seven vehicles in 2020"); Electron launch list [FILING; secondary] |
| 2021 | 6 | 5 | 1 | — | FY2022 10-K ("six vehicles in 2021"); Electron launch list [FILING; secondary] |
| 2022 | 9 | 9 | 0 | — | FY2022 10-K ("nine vehicles in 2022"); Electron launch list [FILING; secondary] |
| 2023 | ~9 orbital + 2 HASTE | 8 orbital + 2 HASTE | 1 orbital | **10** launch missions | FY2025 10-K ("ten launch missions … 2023"); secondary [FILING; secondary] |
| 2024 | ~14 | 14 | 0 | **16** launch missions | FY2025 10-K ("16 launch missions … 2024"); secondary [FILING; secondary] |
| 2025 | ~20 orbital + ~4 HASTE | all successful | 0 | **21** launch missions | FY2025 10-K ("21 launch missions … 2025"); secondary [FILING; secondary] |

**Failures in the window** (all Electron orbital):
- **July 4, 2020** — Flight 13 "Pics Or It Didn't Happen": second-stage anomaly (electrical), payloads lost. [secondary]
- **May 15, 2021** — Flight 20 "Running Out Of Toes": second-stage igniter fault, payloads lost. [secondary]
- **September 19, 2023** — "We Will Never Desert You": a power-system electrical arc in near-vacuum; Capella Space payload lost. [secondary]
- **2022, 2024 and 2025 were 100%-success years.** Rocket Lab's FY2025 press release states the company "flew 21 missions across Electron and HASTE with a 100% success rate for the year."

> **Sourcing caveat on launch counts.** Rocket Lab's own 10-K discloses "launch missions completed" — **10 (2023), 16 (2024), 21 (2025)** — and these figures include HASTE suborbital missions and use Rocket Lab's revenue-recognition definition of a completed mission. Third-party launch trackers (Wikipedia "List of Electron launches," Gunter's Space Page) count *orbital* Electron launches by calendar date and therefore give slightly different per-year numbers, and they differ among themselves on whether to count aborted/scrubbed attempts. The FY2022 10-K's own count — "seven vehicles in 2020, six in 2021, nine in 2022" — is the cleanest primary figure for 2020–2022. For 2023–2025, the 10-K's "launch missions" count (10/16/21) is the figure to use in a Rocket Lab model because it ties directly to Launch Services revenue. Do not mix the two conventions. The exact orbital-vs-suborbital and attempted-vs-successful split for 2024–2025 should be reconciled against the final Electron launch log if launch *count* is a load-bearing model input.

**Operational read:** Electron is a mature, high-cadence small launcher. After a slow 2021 (6 launches, partly pandemic-affected) the cadence roughly tripled by 2025. The lifetime reliability record — three orbital failures spread across 2020, 2021 and 2023, against a large and growing number of successes, with three consecutive 100%-success years where the calendar lines up (2022, 2024, 2025) — is strong for a small-launch vehicle.

## Open Questions

1. **Segment economics below the gross-profit line.** Rocket Lab discloses revenue and gross profit by segment but explicitly does **not** allocate operating expenses, assets, or capex to segments. A valuation that needs Launch-vs-Space *operating margins* or *segment ROIC* must model the opex allocation — there is no sourced figure. (For an orbital-data-center thesis, the relevant comparable is Space Systems, not Launch.)
2. **Non-GAAP gross margin by segment is not published.** Only consolidated non-GAAP gross margin is disclosed. If the model needs an "adjusted" margin for the Space Systems segment specifically, it must be estimated.
3. **Electron launch-count convention.** The 10-K "launch missions" count (10/16/21 for 2023–2025) includes HASTE and uses a revenue-recognition definition; third-party trackers count orbital launches by calendar date. These differ by a few units per year. Reconcile to a single convention before using launch count as a model driver, and pin the exact 2024–2025 orbital/suborbital and attempted/successful split against the final Electron log.
4. **FY2024 backlog segment split** was not extracted from a primary source in this pass (only the FY2025 and FY2022 splits were). If the year-over-year backlog mix matters, pull the FY2024 10-K segment-backlog disclosure.
5. **Convertible notes and true net debt.** The balance sheet carries convertible senior notes and capped-call transactions accounted for under specific standards; "long-term debt" of ~$1.7M (per a secondary summary) understates the convertible obligation. For a valuation, reconstruct the full debt/convertible structure and net-cash position from the FY2025 10-K balance sheet and debt notes.
6. **Neutron has not flown and is pre-revenue.** All revenue and margin history here is Electron + Space Systems. Neutron is the company's medium-lift vehicle and is central to its forward growth narrative (and to any orbital-data-center launch thesis), but it contributed $0 to the FY2020–Q1 2026 results documented here. Neutron development cost is, however, already inside the R&D line dragging operating losses wider.
7. **Acquisition distortion of organic growth.** FY2021–FY2022 growth is heavily inflated by acquisitions (Sinclair, Planetary Systems, SolAero); FY2025–Q1 2026 adds GEOST, Mynaric and (pending) Motiv. The ~76.5% five-year revenue CAGR is **not** an organic growth rate. Use the post-2022 (~42%) CAGR, or build an acquisition-adjusted organic series, for any forward modeling.
8. **Effective tax / NOLs.** Rocket Lab has accumulated large net operating losses across these loss years. The size and usability of the NOL carryforwards (relevant to a DCF terminal value) were not quantified in this baseline and should be pulled from the 10-K tax footnote.
